# Applying to Google AdSense: what Google asks for, and the plan (researched 17 Nov 2026)

**Status: not applied. Nothing in the game loads any ad code or calls Google.** The ad layout (`docs/ADS.md`) is built and switched off. This file is what Google's own help pages say (read on 17 Nov 2026; Google changes them, so re-read before acting), what that means for Minitude, and the order I would do things in. Where a point is my inference and not the page's wording, it says so.

## 1. What Google asks of the site and the person

- **Eligibility** ([answer 9724](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9724)): your own content that is "high-quality, original, and attract an audience"; it complies with the AdSense Program policies; the applicant is **18 or over** (the owner is 24). The page states **no minimum traffic and no minimum site age**. You must have access to the site's HTML source ([answer 91205](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/91205)).
- **Review time** ([7584263](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/7584263), [10162](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/10162), [12170222](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/12170222)): "usually a few days, but in some cases it can take 2-4 weeks". From June 2026 Google may also ask the applicant to verify their identity. Do not remove and re-add the site while it is in review (it can delay it). Site statuses: Requires review, Getting ready, Needs attention, Ready.
- **How a site is added and verified** (7584263): sign in, Add site, enter the URL, then pick ONE verification method and click Verify, then Request review:
  1. the **AdSense code snippet** in the `<head>` of every page that will show ads: `<script async src="https://pagead2.googlesyndication.com/pagead/js/adsbygoogle.js?client=ca-pub-XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX" crossorigin="anonymous"></script>`;
  2. an **ads.txt** line at the site root;
  3. a **meta tag** in the `<head>`: `<meta name="google-adsense-account" content="ca-pub-XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX">`.
  **Why this matters here:** methods 2 and 3 verify the site WITHOUT loading any Google script, so the site can be sent for review before the security policy, the consent banner and the privacy page are changed (those are only needed once ads are actually shown). This is my reading of the listed methods; confirm in the account.
- **ads.txt** ([Google](https://developers.google.com/adsense/platforms/direct/ads-txt)): not a strict requirement, but many advertisers only bid on verified requests. The file lives at the domain root (`https://minitude.io/ads.txt`): `google.com, pub-XXXXXXXXXXXXXXXX, DIRECT, f08c47fec0942fa0` (the publisher id without the `ca-`). It cannot be written until the account exists.

## 2. The policies that matter for a game

(From [Google Publisher Policies, answer 9335564](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9335564), [screens without publisher content, 11112688](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/11112688), [ad placement, 1346295](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/1346295), [Program policies, 48182](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/48182), [make pages ready, 7299563](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/7299563).)

1. **No ads on screens without publisher content, with low-value content, under construction, or "used for alerts, navigation or other behavioral purposes".** Google "may limit or disable ad serving" on such screens. The page does not say whether a game counts as publisher content (a 2024 policy recap I found says it does; the page I read does not). **Risk for us:** the title screen is a screen whose job is the PLAY button. The text pages (`/how-to-play`, `/about`) are the safe place for ads. A "dead end" screen is also banned; the title screen has a way out (PLAY), so it is probably fine.
2. **Accidental clicks.** Be careful with "play buttons", "navigation buttons" and "game windows" next to ads; Google "strongly recommend[s] a distance of at least 150 pixels between the ads and the edge of a game" (written for Flash games, "some games might require a greater distance"), and "even if the layout unintentionally leads to accidental clicks, publishers may still receive a violation notification". Also banned: ads that "overlay or are adjacent to navigational or other action items and may lead to unintended ad interactions". **Our layout leaves 12 to 24 px between the game and an ad, and on a wide window the three title links (top right) and the sound and cabinet buttons (top left) sit about 18 px from the side ads.** This is the biggest policy risk of the layout as built.
3. **No pre-roll:** ads may not be shown "for a preset time before users can view content such as ... games". Never hold PLAY back for an ad.
4. **Other placement rules:** no pop-ups or pop-unders, no "floating box" scripts, ads labelled only "Advertisements" or "Sponsored Links", no arrows or animations that point at the ads, ads must not be mistaken for menu or navigation items, no more ads than content on a screen.
5. **Invalid traffic:** never click your own ads (also while testing: use the dashed preview boxes, not live ads), no robots or automated clicks.
6. **Privacy disclosure:** publishers must "have and abide by a privacy policy that clearly discloses any data collection" (9335564). Google's page on what to say about third parties and cookies was not read: check the current text when writing the new privacy page.
7. **Content quality** (7299563): "unique content that's relevant to your visitors", easy navigation, "valuable and original content is paramount", no scraped or copyrighted content. A privacy page is not listed there but we have one, plus how to play and about pages (17 Nov 2026).

## 3. The security policy (CSP) problem

Google ([answer 16283098](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/16283098)) **supports only a strict, nonce-based policy, not a list of Google's hosts**: `object-src 'none'; script-src 'nonce-{random}' 'unsafe-inline' 'unsafe-eval' 'strict-dynamic' https: http:; base-uri 'none'; report-uri ...`, with the same nonce on EVERY script tag including the AdSense one. "Failure to do so may result in a disruption of ad serving." Test first with `Content-Security-Policy-Report-Only`. The page says nothing about `img-src`, `frame-src`, `connect-src` or `default-src`. **Inference:** the site's `default-src 'self'` (see `docs/SECURITY.md`) would block the ad frames and pictures, and there is no list of hosts that can be added, so on the page with ads these directives would have to be loosened (to `https:`), and the server would have to put a fresh nonce into the game page's HTML on every request (today it serves a static file). The nonce keeps the strongest protection (no injected script can run); the loosening costs the "nothing leaves this site" property. Pages without ads (privacy, how to play, about) keep the strict policy.

## 4. Consent for visitors in Europe

[Answer 13554020](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/13554020), [13554116](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/13554116), [16918505](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/16918505), [7584263](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/7584263): a **certified TCF consent platform is required for personalised ads** in the EEA and UK (since 16 Jan 2024) and Switzerland (since 31 Jul 2024). **Google's own message** (AdSense, Privacy & messaging, "European regulations") is certified, is created automatically when you choose Google's CMP at the review step, appears as a dialog over the site (usually middle or bottom), and is edited in the account; no separate script is mentioned (inferred: it comes with the ad tag). A visitor who declines gets **limited or non-personalised ads, not none** ([14210870](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/14210870)), which still use cookies. It also supports privacy messages for US states (optional). Google "does not check CMPs for full compliance" with privacy law: that remains the owner's responsibility (ask a lawyer if in doubt). The pages describe no way for a visitor to re-open their choice: add a "privacy choices" link ourselves if the account's message does not.

## 5. Children

The site is a general-audience game (the privacy page says it is not directed at children under 13). The pages ([3248194](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/3248194), [17042704](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/17042704)) present the age-treatment tags as optional ("if you'd like us to treat your site as directed to children"): `data-tag-for-age-treatment` on an ad unit (1 = child, 2 = teen, 0 = none), a site-level setting through Search Console, and the new TFAT replacing the older tags. Google "may begin to treat your site ... as child-directed" on its own, and teens' accounts get non-personalised ads automatically. **Inference: no tag is needed to apply.** Tagging the whole site as child-directed would remove personalised ads (less money).

## 6. Money, for a Singapore-based individual

([1709871](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/1709871), [14131950](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/14131950), [7164703](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/7164703).) Identity verification is needed before ads show and payments start (threshold $0); the postal PIN for the address comes at about $10 (S$ equivalent); you can pick a payment method from S$15; **you are paid when you reach S$150** (SGD row of the table); the cycle is monthly (earnings final on the 3rd, payment issued about the 21st to 26th, up to seven business days to arrive). Tax: for Singapore the page asks for a tax residency certificate from the local government and says its status "does not result in account limitations, or impact payouts or withholding tax at the moment". Singapore-billed publishers "may be registered for ... GST". Which payment methods are open to a Singapore address was not stated on the pages read. This is not tax advice.

## 7. The plan

**Phase A: apply (needs the owner, then about 10 minutes of work from me).**
1. The owner creates the AdSense account (Google account, site `https://minitude.io`, country Singapore, account type individual, real name as on the bank account and ID) and gives me the publisher id (`ca-pub-...`, it is public, not a secret).
2. I add the meta tag to the pages (and `public/ads.txt`), run the tests, commit and deploy (the owner's say).
3. The owner clicks Verify, then Request review, and passes identity verification if asked. Wait a few days to four weeks. If it says "Needs attention", read why, fix, and ask for a review again.
Nothing about the game, the privacy page or the security policy needs to change for Phase A (no script, no cookie, no advertising yet).

**Phase B: after the site shows "Ready" (ads on).** Slot ids from the account; the loader that fills the three slots (with the nonce); the nonce and the loosened policy on the game page (Report-Only first, `csp.cjs` must pass); the consent message set up in the account (and a visible privacy-choices link); the privacy page rewritten and re-dated (no longer "no advertising", names Google, cookies, how to opt out, link to Google's partner page); decide the age tag; settle the layout questions below; turn `ADS_ENABLED` on; test on real phones; watch the account for policy messages.

**Decisions only the owner can make before Phase B (they change the design):**
1. **Distance from the game.** As built: 12 to 24 px. Google's guide: 150 px. Following it on a 1440 x 800 laptop shrinks the game from 1088 px wide to about 800 (and on a phone the bottom banner would have to be 150 px from the picture). A cheaper option: side ads only on very large screens, the bottom banner everywhere. Or accept the risk of a violation notice (which can end in ads being limited).
2. **Ads on the text pages too** (how to play, about): the safest place under policy 1 and 2, but they get little traffic.
3. **The links and buttons next to the ads** (point 2 above): they must move away from the ads or the ads from them.

## 8. Honest odds

AdSense gives no traffic or age rule, but rejections for "low value content" and "site isn't ready" are common for new sites whose main page is a picture (third-party reports; Google gives no numbers). Our text is small: three pages. Helps: more real pages (for example a page per question category with what is asked and tips; not copied lists), a few weeks of age, some traffic. A rejection can be fixed and re-applied for; there is no stated penalty (third-party advice: wait two to four weeks and change something real). Income from this site's traffic today would be near nothing whatever the network says (`docs/TRAFFIC.md`).
