## SINGULARITY gems (14 November 2026)

Asked for by the owner over several rounds (name, 120 points, one gem per question, a cabinet, a new sound, the picture); built and deployed on 14 Nov 2026. Choices I made:

- **A gem is not a stored tier.** `answers.tier` has `CHECK (tier BETWEEN 0 AND 5)` and SQLite cannot relax a CHECK without rebuilding a 289,198-row table, so the `gems` table (migration 4) points at the question's gem answer and the checker reports tier 6. The answer keeps its own rarity (the bank sheet and `answers list` still show it).
- **One gem per question, stored with its own one-line description** (`gems.description`), so the answers' descriptions are untouched. The description is what the player sees under the reveal.
- **Perfect run = every answer Mythic or better** (a gem keeps it perfect); the "/ 840" is the most a run can score.
- **The cabinet is in the browser only** (`minitude.gems`), like the logbook: no accounts, nothing on the server. A found gem stores the question, the answer and the line it was shown, so the cabinet needs no server. Hidden slots are counts per category from `GET /api/gems`: the prompts of unfound gems are never sent (a standing rule: prompts only arrive when their question starts).
- **The gem's reveal** is the owner-approved draft one: the roll stops on Mythic, the world is pulled inward (1.4 s) into a black disc, a near-silent beat (0.14 s), then a white flash, a rainbow title "SINGULARITY!", rainbow rays and rings, "+120" and the divide stamp, and the gem flies up into a counter in the top bar. About 1.7 + 1.4 + 4.1 s from the answer to the dive (Mythic: 6.4 s).
- **The counter** ("◆ 3 / 200") appears in the top bar only once the cabinet holds a gem. The cabinet opens from a small button under the SOUND button on the title screen and on the report (hidden during a run).
- **A gem found again** (the same question's gem on another run) still scores 120 and plays the full reveal; the line says "ALREADY IN YOUR CABINET".
- **Multiplayer and the Daily** score gems like any answer (120). In a room the other players see the tier (the Singularity colour) only after the finder's reveal is over, like every tier. The Daily's `items` carry tier 6.
- **Not done on purpose:** no tone variation per category (offered, not asked for); the gem answers were not re-checked for being the right Wikidata item beyond the audit (`gems audit`).

- **Went live on 14 Nov 2026** (owner: "push it to live", all 200 picks approved as they stood): a `db backup` first (`/data/backups/minitude-20261114-135445.db`, 91 MB; disk 266 MB of 974 MB used), `fly deploy --ha=false` (migration 4 ran by itself on start; the 200 questions and 288,508 answers were untouched), `gems import --dry-run`, `gems import` (200 set, 0 skipped), `gems audit` (all good, 34 informational look-alikes, one owner exception), then a live check through the API (a solo run answered with its gem scored tier 6 / 120 points; the run, start and health replies mention no gem), the live page (cabinet button and empty cabinet of 200 slots, no console errors) and the privacy page. The bank sheet was republished with the gems. A rehearsal on a copy of the live database came first.
- **Worth knowing:** "obscure" was measured by how many Wikipedia languages know the answer. Some gems are still easy for English speakers (for "Name a type of bread" the gem is "Everything bagel"); `gems set <question> "<answer>" "<line>"` swaps one, and `data/gems.json` should be kept in step.

## "Everything that counted" on the Daily's report (14 November 2026)

Asked for by the owner: click the answer you gave on a Daily question and see ALL accepted answers for it, by rarity, with the number in each rarity, "even if I am revealing the singularity answer"; no black box. Choices:

- **Timing and who:** shown right away, to the guest who finished that Daily (the owner chose this over holding the names until midnight UTC, knowing that early finishers could pass the answers on or clear their browser data and replay with them). Daily only; solo and multiplayer were offered and declined for now.
- **Server:** `GET /api/daily/answers?day&index` (counts per rarity, the total, the gem) and `GET /api/daily/answers/list?day&index&tier|q&offset&limit` (a page, A to Z, 100 by default, 200 at most; `q` searches names, normalised names and curated aliases across all rarities, 2 to 60 characters). It needs the guest header, a played and FINISHED Daily of that day (it settles an abandoned one first), and the same per-network request limit as the rest. 403 `not_finished`, 404 `no_play`.
- **Counting:** the gem is counted under SINGULARITY (1) and taken out of its stored rarity, like in the game. The counts always add up to every accepted answer. "Answers" are the canonical answers; curated aliases are shown under each as "also: ..." and found by search; spellings learned from typos are not shown.
- **Screen:** an HTML panel over the game (`answers.ts`), because a long list with search and paging does not fit the canvas: a click on a report row (or the keys 1 to 7) opens it; each rarity is a row with its count, opening one loads its names; the player's own rarity and answer are marked; the gem row is open from the start with its one-line story; a question that timed out can be opened too ("The time ran out on this question"). Esc or CLOSE closes it. Names load a page at a time: the biggest question (fish, 15,138 answers, almost all Mythic) answers in under 20 ms.
- **Not changed:** the privacy page (nothing new is stored or sent about the player).

## The reading font of the panels, and the rank statistic (14 November 2026)

- **Font:** the answers screen ("everything that counted") and the gem cabinet showed their reading text (question, "You answered...", notes, search box, the accepted answers and their "also:" lines) in the browser's plain sans-serif, which the owner found disconnected from the pixel look. Six pixel fonts were compared on the real panel with real answers (a comparison page, deleted once the choice was made; Pixelify Sans, VT323, DotGothic16, Tiny5, Jersey 10, Micro 5) and the owner chose **Pixelify Sans**, the game's own text font (`PixelifyFix-*.ttf`, already in `public/fonts`). `--read` in `site.css` is the font stack; sizes were raised by a pixel (base 16, question 23, search 17). The page under the report, the privacy page and the small pixel labels are unchanged. Every candidate drew the common accented letters (7.8% of accepted answers contain a non-ASCII letter); the few rare letters Pixelify lacks (some Vietnamese and Arabic ones) fall back to the plain font.
- **Rank:** the logbook on the page under the report has a fifth statistic, the rank of this run's score among **every finished solo and Daily run on record** ("#3, of 14 dives, top 21%"). Chosen defaults: it counts **dives, not people** (there are no accounts, so one person's many dives all count); a run is counted only when all seven questions have a result (a run left half way is not); equal scores share a rank (rank = 1 + the number of dives that scored MORE); the percentage ("top N%", rounded up) appears only from 20 dives on; a multiplayer match is not stored, so its score is ranked as if it were one more dive (`mine` = 0); a reopened Daily result and a fresh run are already in the count (`mine` = 1). It is a row of its own under the four older statistics (the number can be long), and it is simply not shown if the server cannot be reached. The Daily's own "rank among today's players" on the report is unchanged. The server counts with one query over `run_questions` (about 100 ms at 100,000 dives); a cached table is the next step if the game ever grows that large. ## Security hardening (16 November 2026)

Asked by the owner: "I want to ensure that the application is secure ... attackers cannot attack my application ... I need your input on this." The full list of what was tried, found and left is `docs/SECURITY.md`. Two findings were serious and are fixed: an 80 MB request body took the server's memory from 83 MB to 2.3 GB (the live machine has 512 MB), and a flood of new solo runs could fill the disk (about 1 KB each, so about 140 MB a day from one address; the per-address limit did not apply to a visitor with IPv6, who has a new address on every request). Choices made without asking (change freely, but tell the owner):
1. The API refuses a request body over **8 KB** (`MAX_BODY_BYTES`, answer 413). Real requests are a few hundred bytes.
2. **No CORS header** (it was open to every website). Only the game's own pages call the API.
3. IPv6 visitors are counted by their **/64** (`networkOf`), for every limit, and the stored per-day fingerprint hashes the network, not the address. A household behind one /64 shares the limits, as a household behind one IPv4 address always did.
4. New solo runs: at most **20,000 a day** for the whole server (`MAX_RUNS_PER_DAY`, 0 = off) and refused below **150 MB** free disk; new Daily runs refused below **40 MB**; the nightly backup is skipped below **60 MB** spare after the copy. The reply is "We are very busy right now. Please try again in a little while." (it says nothing about disks or databases). Solo runs left half way for **6 hours** are deleted every hour (the rank statistic does not count unfinished runs; Daily runs are never touched).
5. Every reply carries a **Content-Security-Policy** that allows only the site's own address (inline styles only; no inline script), `X-Frame-Options: DENY` and a Permissions-Policy that switches off camera, microphone, location and the other unused features.
6. Answers have control characters turned into spaces before they are stored (the owner reads them in a terminal); an unknown `/api/...` path is a JSON 404.
7. Dependencies: `hono` 4.13.18 and `@hono/node-server` 2.1.8 (major version; `npm audit --omit=dev` reports 0).
8. (Added the same day, after a review of the above found three gaps, each shown with a script.) **300 new solo runs a day per network** (`MAX_RUNS_PER_NETWORK_PER_DAY`), so one person cannot spend the whole server's 20,000. **Start / miss / abandon are rate limited** (3 times the answer route's 90 a minute per network; they had no limit, and a script made 300 finished junk runs in 4.7 s with 8 requests each). **Solo runs older than 24 hours in which nothing was typed and nothing scored are deleted** every hour (`purgeEmptyRuns`); the only visible effect is that such zero-point runs stop counting in the "how you rank" figure. Daily runs are never deleted by any of these.
9. **Every limit is a setting** (`MAX_RUNS_PER_DAY`, `MAX_RUNS_PER_NETWORK_PER_DAY`, `DAILY_PER_IP`, `RATE_LIMIT_PER_MINUTE`, 0 = off) so they can be relaxed in `fly.toml` as the game grows: `docs/SECURITY.md`, "When you scale". The owner's words: the measures will be relaxed "should I choose to scale".
Not done (listed with reasons in `docs/SECURITY.md`): a per-network cap on multiplayer rooms, memory for each question's spellings, a non-root container, the development tools' own advisories, a retention period for finished runs, an alarm when a guard trips, the owner's two-step sign-in on their accounts.
