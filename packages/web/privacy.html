How Minitude works

A run

A run is seven questions. Each one gives you a category and a prompt, such as “Name a breed of dog”, and you have 30 seconds to type any valid answer. There is no single right answer: any real one counts.

The rarer your answer, the more it scores, and the smaller you become:

  • Common 10
  • Uncommon 20
  • Rare 40
  • Exotic 60
  • Legendary 80
  • Mythic 100
  • Singularity 120

Singularity is the gem. Every question hides exactly one gem: a very rare answer that scores 120 points and shrinks you more than anything else. Gems are not announced in advance, so finding one takes luck and curiosity.

You start at human size, about 1.7 metres. Every point shrinks you further, through worlds of the very small, down towards the Planck length, the smallest distance that physics can meaningfully talk about. Seven Mythic answers make 700 points, and the most a run can score is 840, with a gem in every question. Getting all the way to the bottom is rare, and we will not spoil what happens there.

Slips, retries and running out of time

If what you type is very close to a real answer, the game may ask “Did you mean …?”. You answer yes or no, and the clock keeps running while it asks. An answer that does not count can be tried again while there is time left. If time runs out on a question you score nothing for it and you do not shrink.

If you think a correct answer was turned down, or you spot a mistake in a question, please tell us.

The Daily Challenge

Everyone gets the same seven questions each day, and the day changes at midnight UTC. You have one attempt per day, and closing the page ends your run. Afterwards you can see roughly where you ranked among that day’s players. Standings are anonymous, and your streak counts the days in a row you finished the Daily.

Multiplayer rooms

Two to four players can play the same seven questions together in a room, either private (you share its code with friends) or public (anyone can join from the list of rooms). Players choose a display name for the room.

Your gem cabinet

Every gem you find is kept in your gem cabinet, which you can open from the title screen and from the end-of-run report. For each gem it keeps the question you were asked, the answer you gave and the one line the game showed about it. It is kept only in your browser: we cannot see it, and clearing this site’s data empties it.

Your logbook

The logbook that appears when a run ends shows your streak (the number of days in a row on which you finished a run of any kind), how many runs you have played, your average score and your best score. It is kept only in your browser: we cannot see it.

What we store, and why

There are no accounts. We never ask for your name, email address or location, and the game contains no advertising, no tracking cookies and no third-party analytics. It does count visits, in a way that cannot tell one visitor from another (see “A visit count” below). This is what the game does keep on its own servers:

What stays in your browser

Minitude sets no cookies. It keeps five small items in your browser’s local storage, and nothing else:

  • minitude.guest: a random ID made up by your browser the first time you play. It is how the Daily knows it is you again. It is not linked to your name or anything else about you.
  • minitude.logbook: your logbook: runs played, average, best and the days you played.
  • minitude.gems: your gem cabinet: the gems you have found, each with its question, your answer and a line about it.
  • minitude.muted: whether you turned the sound off.
  • minitude.name: the last display name you used in a multiplayer room.

You can remove all of it at any time by clearing this site’s data in your browser’s settings.

Who else is involved

  • Hosting. The game runs on servers from Fly.io, in Singapore. When you connect, their network necessarily sees your network address and handles the connection, as any web host does, and may keep ordinary operational logs. Fly.io’s own privacy policy applies to that.
  • The domain name. Our domain name is managed by Cloudflare, used for name lookups only. Visits to the game do not pass through Cloudflare.
  • Nobody else. We do not sell your information, and we do not share it with advertisers. We would disclose information only if the law required us to.

Links to other sites, such as our Instagram page, take you away from Minitude, and their own policies apply there.

Your choices and rights

Because there are no accounts, we cannot look up “your” data by name. The only thing that ties anything to you is the anonymous ID above, so if you want what is held against your browser’s ID removed, or you want to know what it is, contact us and quote that ID. Depending on where you live, you may have legal rights over information about you, such as the right to see it, correct it or have it deleted, and we will respect them.

We keep game records for as long as they are useful for running and improving Minitude, and we delete or anonymise them when they are not. Clearing your browser’s data removes everything Minitude keeps on your device.

Children

Minitude is a general-audience game. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. We do not ask anyone for personal details, and we ask that nobody types them into the game.
