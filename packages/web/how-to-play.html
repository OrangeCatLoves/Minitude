How to play Minitude

Minitude is a trivia game with no single right answer. You get a prompt such as “Name a breed of dog”, you type any real answer you can think of, and the rarer your answer is, the smaller you shrink. The goal is to get as small as you can, from human size down towards the smallest distance in physics.

A run

A run is seven questions. Each one shows a category (Animals, Food, Games, History, Movies and TV, Music, Places, Science, Sports, Transport and more) and a prompt, and you have 30 seconds to type an answer. You do not pick from a list of choices: you type whatever you can think of, and any real answer that fits the prompt counts.

When you answer, the game shows you how rare your answer was and how small it made you. Then the next question starts, and when the seventh is done you see a report with your points, your final size and how you did.

Scoring

Every answer is worth points according to how rare it is, from the one everybody thinks of first to the one almost nobody does:

  • Common 10
  • Uncommon 20
  • Rare 40
  • Exotic 60
  • Legendary 80
  • Mythic 100
  • Singularity 120

The Singularity is a hidden gem. Every question has exactly one gem: a very rare answer that is worth more than any other and shrinks you further than anything else. Nothing tells you what the gem is in advance. If you find one it goes into your gem cabinet, which you can open from the title screen.

Seven Mythic answers make a perfect 700, and a run that finds a gem in every question can reach 840.

Getting small

You start at human size, about 1.7 metres tall. Every point you score makes you smaller: every 20 points shrinks you to a tenth of your size. Each time that happens the picture takes you into a new world, a scale ten times smaller than the last, from everyday objects to specks of dust, cells, molecules, atoms and then sizes that nobody has ever measured.

Far enough down you leave the part of the map that is known and reach the end of what physics can describe, the Planck length. Reaching it takes a run of very rare answers, and the game keeps its surprises for whoever gets there.

Typing, slips and time

  • Spelling. You do not have to be perfect. If what you type is very close to a real answer, the game asks “Did you mean …?” and you say yes or no. The clock keeps running while it asks, and saying no means your text is judged exactly as you typed it.
  • Other ways of writing an answer. Hyphens and spaces, British and American spellings, “Mt” for “Mount”, and Roman or ordinary numerals are not a problem.
  • Groups count. A word that stands for a whole group of valid answers, such as “rattlesnake” for venomous snakes, is accepted as a Common answer. The word that is in the prompt itself (for example “snake”) is not.
  • Second tries. An answer that does not count can be replaced with another while there is time left on the clock.
  • Running out of time. If the 30 seconds run out you score nothing for that question and you do not shrink, but the run goes on.

Ways to play

The Daily Challenge

Everyone in the world gets the same seven questions each day, and the day changes at midnight UTC. You have one attempt per day and closing the page ends your run, so pick a moment when you can play it through. Afterwards you can see roughly where you ranked among that day’s players (standings are anonymous), and the game counts your streak of days in a row. After the Daily you can also look at everything that counted for each question.

Solo run

Play as many runs as you like, as often as you like, with seven questions picked for you. Nothing is tied to an account, and your logbook of streak, runs played, average and best score is kept in your own browser.

Multiplayer

Two to four players can play the same seven questions together in a room: a private room that you share by code with friends, or a public one that anyone can join. When it is over you see how everyone did.

Tips

  • The first answer that comes into your head comes into everyone else’s too, so it scores the least. If you have time, think of something more specific.
  • A safe answer scored quickly beats no answer at all. You can always try a rarer one if the clock allows.
  • The prompt tells you what counts. “Name a Game Boy game” accepts any Game Boy game, big or small, and rewards the ones few people remember.
  • Use the Daily for a fair comparison with other people, and solo runs to practise.
  • Press Enter to send your answer.

Common questions

Is Minitude free?

Yes, and it needs no account or sign-up. There is nothing to download: it runs in your browser on a computer, tablet or phone.

My answer was right and it was not accepted. What do I do?

Tell us. Use the contact link below or the box under the report at the end of a run. Real answers that we missed are added.

Can I suggest a question?

Yes. When a run ends, the page under the report has a box for your idea.

What happens to my information?

Very little is kept, and none of it is a name or an email address. The privacy page lists exactly what, and why.

Where can I read more about the game?

The about page says what Minitude is and how it came about.
